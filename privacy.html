Privacy Policy

Effective November 1, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 (added “How your data is protected”)

The short version: SquadWatch runs entirely on your computer. If you sign in with Google, it reads your YouTube subscriptions to show you who's live. If you also turn on chatting, it posts the chat messages you type — and nothing else. Your data is never sent to the developer, never sold, never shared and never used for ads. You can remove it at any time.

1. About SquadWatch

SquadWatch is a free desktop app for macOS and Windows that plays up to four YouTube live streams side by side. It is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by YouTube or Google. This policy explains what data SquadWatch accesses, how it is used and stored, and the choices you have.

2. SquadWatch uses YouTube API Services

SquadWatch uses YouTube API Services to show videos, live chats, your subscriptions and channel information. By using SquadWatch, you agree to be bound by the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's handling of data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

3. What data SquadWatch accesses

You can use SquadWatch without signing in (by pasting stream links). If you choose Sign in with Google, you are asked to grant these permissions:

Without chatting turned on, SquadWatch's access is read-only. With chatting turned on, the only thing SquadWatch ever does in your account is post a live chat message, and only when you type it and press Send. It never uploads, comments, likes, subscribes, unsubscribes, deletes or changes anything else in your YouTube or Google account, and it never posts anything on its own. Google describes the chatting permission broadly ("Manage your YouTube account") because YouTube has no narrower permission for sending chat messages.

4. How the data is used

  • Only to show it to you, inside the app, for the features described above.
  • Not used for advertising, profiling, analytics or any other purpose.
  • Not sold, rented or shared with anyone, including the developer of SquadWatch.
  • Not used to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.
  • Not shown to anyone other than you.

SquadWatch's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

5. Where data is stored

SquadWatch has no servers. Everything stays on your computer:

  • Sign-in token — a token from Google that keeps you signed in is saved in SquadWatch's settings folder on your computer. It is deleted when you sign out or delete your data.
  • Chat messages you send — go straight from your computer to YouTube and are not stored by SquadWatch. YouTube shows them in the stream's public live chat like any other message. SquadWatch keeps only a count of how many messages you've sent today (to apply its daily limit) on your computer.
  • Subscriptions, live status and channel information — kept only in the app's memory while it is running (channel pages are cached for a few minutes), and discarded when you close the app or sign out.
  • Your layout and saved "squads" — the streams on screen and groups you save are kept in the app's local storage on your computer. Channel names and pictures in saved squads are refreshed from YouTube whenever you're signed in, and are removed automatically if they haven't been refreshed for 30 days.

6. How your data is protected

SquadWatch is built so that your Google and YouTube data stays private and under your control:

  • No servers, no copies. SquadWatch has no backend. Your data never passes through or is stored on any server run by the developer; the developer cannot see, access or recover it.
  • Encrypted connections. All communication with Google and YouTube uses HTTPS (TLS encryption). The app's own interface is served only to your own computer (localhost) and is not reachable from the network.
  • Secure sign-in. You sign in on Google's own page in your web browser, using OAuth 2.0 with PKCE. SquadWatch never sees or stores your Google password.
  • Short-lived access tokens. The access token used to call the YouTube Data API is kept only in memory and expires within an hour.
  • Protected sign-in token. The token that keeps you signed in is stored only on your computer, in SquadWatch's settings folder inside your user account, with file permissions that allow only your user account to read it (on macOS the file is readable only by you; on Windows it is in your private user profile folder). It is revoked with Google and deleted when you sign out or choose “Delete my data”.
  • Least privilege. SquadWatch requests read-only YouTube access by default. The permission to post chat messages is requested only if you choose “Turn on chatting”, and is used only to send messages you type.
  • Minimal retention. Subscription and channel data is held only in memory while the app runs and is discarded when you close it or sign out. Saved squad information that hasn't been refreshed for 30 days is removed automatically.
  • No third parties. No analytics, advertising or tracking services are built into SquadWatch, and your data is never sold, shared or transferred to anyone.
  • Reporting a problem. If you believe you've found a security or privacy issue, please report it through the contact link below; it will be investigated and fixed promptly.

7. Data sent to Google and YouTube

To work, SquadWatch connects directly from your computer to Google and YouTube: to sign you in, to read your subscriptions and channel information through the YouTube Data API (and, if you turned on chatting, to post the messages you send), and to play videos and show live chat through YouTube's embedded player. The embedded player and chat are provided by YouTube, may use cookies or similar technologies, and are covered by the Google Privacy Policy. SquadWatch itself uses no cookies for tracking, no analytics and no advertising.

8. Revoking access and deleting your data

  • Sign out (account menu in the sidebar): immediately revokes SquadWatch's access with Google and deletes the sign-in token and your subscription data from the app.
  • Delete my data (account menu): does the above and also erases everything SquadWatch saved on your computer — layout, squads and settings — right away.
  • Turning chatting off: sign out (which revokes all of SquadWatch's access) and sign in again without clicking “Turn on chatting”. Messages you already posted are part of YouTube's chat and can be managed on YouTube.
  • From your Google account: you can remove SquadWatch's access at any time on Google's security settings page. The next time SquadWatch opens, it detects this, signs you out and deletes your subscription data and sign-in token.
  • Uninstalling: deleting the app and its settings folder removes everything it stored.

Because SquadWatch stores nothing outside your computer, the developer holds no copy of your data to delete. Deleting SquadWatch's data does not affect anything in your YouTube or Google account.

9. Children

SquadWatch is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them.

10. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If a change affects how your Google or YouTube data is used, SquadWatch will ask you to review and accept the updated policy before continuing to use those features.
